How Los Angeles Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A capacitor that's starting to fail is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected for a season, can take out the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement.
